About This Project

When I first got into miniature painting, I found it - forgive the pun - quite opaque. As I started out, I learned about the hobby as I imagine most people do - haphazardly, picking up skills, knowledge and information from youtube videos, reddit comments, Warhammer beginner sets and advice from discords. I would see advice bandied about like “thin your paints” or “two thin coats”, or how zenithal highlights and contrast paints are cheat codes for good looking mini painting, but trying to find information on how or why those things worked was something of a crapshoot. Folks on youtube and reddit would sling around terms like “NMM” or “OSL” or “wet blending” freely, without meaningful context or explanation.

For many folks, whose interest in miniature painting begins and ends with making good-looking enough minis in a short enough timeframe to have them ready by the next game night, this sort of haphazard cheat-sheet style information is more than enough. But for me, it made me feel frustrated and lost - I didn`t understand any of the tools or techniques beginners videos were saying I should use, and building a comprehensive knowledge of the hobby felt exhausting, sifting through dozens of videos and comments from hundreds of creators. I didn`t understand what was possible in the hobby, or how I could meaningfully make personal, creative decisions when painting beyond what color the space marine`s armor trim should be.

It wasn`t until several months into my time with the hobby, when I picked up an actual art textbook (James Gurney`s Color and Light, a book you will see referenced often here), that I began to understand how all the various aspects of miniature painting are situated within the broader context of painting at large - how techniques like OSL and NMM are exercises in understanding chroma and value, or how washes tint the hue of a surface. Having that understanding gave me an appreciation, understanding, and passion for the hobby that I hadn`t had up to that point.

This website, then, is made for people like me when I first started learning. Its goals are threefold:

  1. To contextualize commonly used terms and techniques in the miniature painting community, both in terms of what they mean, and also what knowledge is necessary or helpful to understand and execute those techniques.
  2. To situate miniature painting in the context of painting at large. People have been painting for a lot longer than miniature painting has been around, and a lot of their knowledge is useful and applicable for our hobby. Understanding how the two relate allows you to draw more information and inspiration from a greater variety of sources.
  3. To compile references where you can go to learn more about these terms and techniques, and their practical application.

My hope, then, is that this can be a useful reference to keep by your side as you navigate the world of miniature painting - to look up terms you don`t understand, to address blind spots in your knowledge, and to better grasp the variety of tools available to you in this hobby and all the things that are possible within it. I hope that I can give you the tools and understanding to help you love miniature painting as much as I do.


-TK Haughton
